## Overview

Class Connect tracks daily attendance without using class time. A teacher wanted attendance recorded every day
without taking minutes out of the lesson, so students tap a fob on a small scanner, and the scan is sent to the
Class Connect website.

I was one of four people on the team. My part was the electronics, the 3D modelling and some of the website
design. It was built and tested with more than 200 students and several cards, and each unit costs about $20 in
parts against a budget target of $50 to $100.

## How it works

1. A student taps an RFID fob on the scanner. The fobs are ETEKJOY 13.56 MHz MIFARE Classic 1K compatible
   fobs, bought as a 100-pack for $18.
2. An RC522-style reader module reads the fob and passes it to an **ESP32 DevKit V1** over SPI.
3. The ESP32 sends the scan to the Class Connect website over **Bluetooth LE**.
4. The ESP32 drives three outputs: a **green LED** (GPIO2) when a card has checked in, a **red LED** (GPIO25)
   for an error, and a **speaker** (GPIO26) for audio feedback.

The scanner is powered by USB only, with no battery. The cable enters through an opening in the base wall, and
the reader runs from the DevKit's 3V3 pin.

## The hardware

The board is two copper layers, 72.37 × 77.14 mm and 1.5 mm thick, designed in Fusion Electronics with a
separate schematic. The ESP32 DevKit is mounted on the bottom side, the LEDs and speaker are wired to the
ESP32's GPIO pins, and four Ø2.5 mm holes mount the reader module.

| Ref | Part | Qty | Role |
| --- | ---- | --- | ---- |
| U3 | ESP32 DevKit V1 (ESP32-WROOM-32), 30-pin dev board | 1 | Microcontroller with Bluetooth LE and USB power |
| U2 | CEG006900, 8-pin SPI module | 1 | RC522-style 13.56 MHz RFID reader |
| D1 | Green LED, 5 mm radial | 1 | Card checked in |
| D2 | Red LED, 5 mm radial | 1 | Error |
| SP1 | Speaker, Adafruit AL60P footprint | 1 | Audio feedback |

## The enclosure

The case is two printed parts, a base and a lid, with a footprint of 89.2 × 96.5 mm and a stacked height of about
17 mm. The base has a 2 mm floor.

- The lid has two LED holes, Ø6.45 mm and Ø6.29 mm, that line up with the green and red LEDs (D1 and D2).
- A Ø13.48 mm opening in the lid sits in line with the speaker (SP1).
- One 13.7 mm-wide opening in the base wall lets the USB cable reach the ESP32.

It was printed in PLA on a Bambu Lab A1 Mini. The STEP and STL files are in the downloads above.

## Engineering story

**Design decisions**

- Switched from an Arduino to an ESP32.
- Used separate blank RFID fobs instead of scanning student ID barcodes, because of FERPA and privacy concerns.
- Chose the $18 pack of 100 ETEKJOY fobs over a $40 pack of blank cards.

**What went wrong**

Some parts were soldered on wrong. Because the team had spare parts, they were replaced and fixed. A concern
from the teacher was that students lose their fobs.

**Testing**

The scanner handled more than 200 students and multiple cards.

**What I'd change**

Our planning, and how hard we worked each day.
